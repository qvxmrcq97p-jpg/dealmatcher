## Day 1 — Florida insurance crisis: how to underwrite it correctly

If you're buying a Florida buy-and-hold right now, your insurance assumption is probably the single largest source of model error.

The headline: average homeowners insurance in Florida hit ~$6,000/yr in 2024 — roughly 4x the national average. For non-owner-occupied investment properties (no homestead protection), expect another 30-50% on top.

What's driving it: hurricane risk repricing, fewer carriers writing policies, and Citizens Insurance (the state-backed insurer of last resort) having to absorb risk that private carriers won't take.

How to underwrite it for a typical $300K SFR:

- **Insurance:** $7,500-9,500/yr (1-bath, masonry, post-2002 build, inland)
- **Coastal counties (Miami-Dade, Broward, Pinellas):** add 25-40%
- **Pre-1995 wood-frame:** add 30%
- **Open claim within last 5 years:** likely uninsurable except via Citizens

Practical move: get a binder quote BEFORE you contract, not after. Most flips and assignments fall apart at the binder stage now, not the inspection. Use a Florida-licensed broker who writes with multiple carriers — Tower Hill, Heritage, Frontline, Citizens.

The deals that survive in this market are the ones where you've already priced realistic insurance into the offer.

## Day 2 — The 70% rule applied to TODAY's market

Classic formula: Maximum Allowable Offer = (ARV × 0.70) - Repair Costs.

The 70% multiplier accounts for: holding costs, transaction fees, profit margin (~10-15%), and a buffer for surprises. It's a quick screen, not a precise underwriter.

Let's run it on a real deal from yesterday's scrape:

- **Address:** 11841 SW 213th St, Miami, 33177
- **Asking:** $245,000
- **ARV (recent comps):** $385,000
- **Estimated repairs:** $40,000

Calculation: $385,000 × 0.70 = $269,500 - $40,000 = **$229,500 MAO**

Asking is $245K. That's $15.5K over MAO at the 70% screen. Could you make this work? Possibly — if comps are conservative and repairs trim down. But it's not a slam-dunk at asking.

Where the rule breaks: high-end markets ($600K+ ARV) where 70% is too aggressive (use 75%); BRRRR plays where you'll refi out of holding costs (can stretch to 75-80%); turnkey rentals where there's no ARV-uplift component (use cap rate instead).

The rule is a fast filter to kill bad deals in 30 seconds. If a wholesaler tries to push above 70%, ask them why. Their answer reveals whether it's a real deal or a stretch.

## Day 4 — Save-Our-Homes 2026: what changed for investors

Florida's homestead exemption rules tightened in early 2026. Here's the relevant updates for investment property buyers:

- **Cap on assessed-value increase:** still 3% per year for homesteaded properties; 10% for non-homesteaded (you).
- **Portability:** owner-occupant homestead transfer has been raised in some counties; doesn't help investors directly but shifts the secondary buyer pool.
- **Assignment caps:** for non-homesteaded properties, the 10% cap is per-county; if you're scattering rentals across multiple counties, this matters when modeling 5-year tax growth.

Practical impact on a typical $300K rental:

- Year 1 taxes (post-purchase): ~$5,400
- Year 5 taxes (max appreciation): ~$8,000
- That's ~$2,600 over 5 years that affects your IRR by ~0.4-0.6%

Most investor underwriting models I see still assume 2% tax growth. With the new caps + appreciation in FL, plan on 5-8% tax growth annually for non-homesteaded. Build that into your spreadsheet or your DSCR-loan-ready proforma will look better than reality.

For active investors: you can still drive long-run returns by buying properties where the seller's prior assessment cap has reset (fresh sale = re-assessed at market) and the new cap protects YOU going forward.

## Day 7 — Cap rate in a 7%+ rate environment

When mortgage rates were 3.5%, a 5% cap rate could cash-flow a leveraged rental. Today at 7.25%, that math breaks.

**Quick formula:** for a leveraged rental to cash-flow, your cap rate should be roughly mortgage rate + 1-2 points.

So:
- 7% mortgage → minimum 8.5-9% cap
- 6.5% mortgage → minimum 8% cap
- 8% mortgage → minimum 9.5-10% cap (if such a deal exists)

For Florida this means SFRs in Miami-Dade and Palm Beach — typically 4-5% caps — don't cash-flow at retail with a conventional loan. You need to either:
1. Buy below market (wholesale)
2. Pay cash (kills the leverage benefit)
3. Use creative financing (seller-finance, sub-to)

Where 8.5%+ caps still exist in FL:
- Off-market SFRs in Hillsborough, Polk, Volusia, Marion counties
- Small multifamily (2-4 unit) in St. Pete, Lakeland, Daytona
- Workforce housing zips ($150-225K SFRs renting $1,800-2,400)

Don't chase 5% cap deals expecting "rates will come back down." Underwrite to the rate you can lock today. If the deal works at 7.5%, it works regardless.

## Day 8 — 1031 exchange timing: stacking closings

The two big timeline rules:
- **Day 45:** identify replacement property in writing
- **Day 180:** close on identified property

What kills most 1031s isn't a bad replacement — it's running out of time. Here's how to game the calendar:

**Strategy 1 — Pre-identify multiple replacement candidates.** You can identify up to 3 properties (or unlimited if total value <200% of relinquished). Identify all 3 by Day 45 even if you only intend to close on 1.

**Strategy 2 — Reverse exchange (more expensive but bulletproof).** Close on the replacement BEFORE selling the relinquished. Requires QI to take title, costs ~$3-5K extra, but eliminates the timing risk entirely. Worth it on $500K+ exchanges.

**Strategy 3 — Sell late in the year.** A November 15 closing on the relinquished gives you Day 180 in early May — outside hurricane season, normal closing market. A May 15 close puts your Day 180 in mid-November when many sellers slow down.

**Strategy 4 — Use a 1031 of multiple properties.** Sell two $300K rentals, exchange into one $600K property. The math works if cash basis on the new exceeds combined basis on old.

Always work with a Qualified Intermediary you've used before. Don't try to DIY a 1031 — the IRS forms are unforgiving. (This isn't tax advice — talk to your CPA.)

## Day 9 — How wholesalers price assignments

When a wholesaler quotes you a deal, they've baked in their fee. Understanding the math helps you spot a good price vs. a stretched one.

**Typical assignment fee structure:**
- Floor: $5,000 (deals under $150K, low-margin)
- Median: $10-15K (most $150K-300K deals)
- Premium: $20-40K (deals with strong ARV margin or motivated buyer pool)
- Stretch: $50K+ (rare; usually high-end SFRs or comm'l, often won't close)

**The 4 variables that determine assignment price:**

1. **Spread between contract price and ARV.** If wholesaler has it at $200K and ARV is $350K, there's $150K of spread before repairs. Wholesaler captures $30-50K of that.

2. **Days under contract.** If they've held it 25 days and inspection contingencies are about to expire, they're motivated to assign at any price.

3. **Motivated seller's flexibility.** Cash close in 7 days at 80% of asking? That's a $30K+ assignment. 30 days at 90%? Probably $10K.

4. **Buyer pool size.** If the property is generic and 50 buyers want it, premium assignment. If it's a niche (mobile homes, water-damaged), discount.

**Red flags:**
- Wholesaler refuses to share contract price (they're hiding their margin)
- ARV provided is not backed by 3+ comps within 0.5 miles
- "Cash only, 7 days, no inspections" pushed before you've even seen the property

**Green flags:**
- Wholesaler proactively shares the executed contract
- Inspection contingency window remains
- They're willing to assign for a fixed dollar amount (not %)

## Day 10 — Florida lien search: what to check before contracting

Before you sign that wholesale assignment, run these 3 lookups in 15 minutes:

**1. County Clerk: Open Mortgages, Liens, Judgments**

Search by owner name + address at your county clerk's website (every FL county has one online). Look for:
- Active mortgages (sometimes multiple)
- Mechanic's liens (contractor work unpaid)
- Judgment liens (unpaid lawsuits)
- HOA / condo association liens (often hidden, attaches to property)

**2. Code Enforcement: Open Violations**

Most counties expose this online: Hillsborough → "Code Enforcement Search," Miami-Dade → "Code Compliance Action." You're looking for:
- Open citations (you inherit these)
- Daily-fine violations that are accruing
- Prior fines paid (signal of trouble)

**3. Tax Roll: Delinquencies + Special Assessments**

County tax collector site. Search by parcel ID. Check:
- Tax certificates (someone paid the back taxes already; they get paid off at closing — usually fine)
- Tax deeds (more serious — property is in tax-deed sale path)
- Special assessments (CDD/MUD/sewer/sidewalk improvement charges; can be $200-2,000/yr surprise)

Total time: 15 minutes per property. Catches 80% of "surprise at closing" issues. The deals that fall apart in escrow almost always have one of these in their backstory.

If a wholesaler hasn't done this work for you, it's a sign they're newer / less experienced. Not disqualifying, but adjust your expectations.

## Day 12 — FHA/VA finisher opportunities

Most flips are sold for cash or to conventional buyers. There's a smaller-but-overlooked exit: cosmetic flips priced for FHA/VA buyers.

**Why this matters:**

FHA/VA buyers are 35-40% of all FL home purchases under $400K. They have lower down payments (3.5% / 0%), so they can buy when conventional buyers can't. This means your buyer pool 2-3x as large at the right price points.

**What qualifies for FHA/VA:**
- Roof life: minimum 2 years remaining
- All systems functional: HVAC, plumbing, electrical
- No major structural issues (foundation, settling, termite)
- Lead paint disclosure if pre-1978 (and remediated if any)
- Clean title

**What doesn't qualify (VA stricter than FHA):**
- Open mechanic's liens
- Active code violations
- Roof past usable life
- Properties with unresolved permit issues

**The play:**

Cosmetic flips ($30-50K rehab, no structural). Replace flooring, kitchen, baths, paint, fix obvious cosmetic — but DON'T do expensive structural work that won't return the cost. Price at $260-340K (sweet spot for FHA in FL).

You'll get 8-15 offers in 2 weeks. Most will be FHA. Yours converts at higher rate than cash-only listings.

This isn't sexy, but it's the most reliable exit strategy in 2026.

## Day 13 — Tax assessor vs. real ARV: closing the gap

Most investors I talk to use tax-assessor value as a proxy for ARV. That's a 2-4 year-old number.

**What's actually happening with assessor values in 2026:**

- Florida property values jumped 30-45% across many counties from 2021-2024
- Assessor values lag market by 1-3 years
- For a property assessed at $250K, real ARV is often $310-340K
- For a property assessed at $400K, real ARV may be $480-510K

**Quick way to estimate real ARV without paying for an appraisal:**

1. Get the parcel's tax-assessed value
2. Pull the most recent sold comp within 0.5 miles, similar size + age
3. Compare comp's tax-assessed value to comp's actual sold price
4. Apply that ratio to your subject

Example:
- Subject parcel assessed: $260K
- Comp parcel assessed: $245K, sold for $385K
- Ratio: 385 / 245 = 1.57
- Your subject estimated ARV: $260K × 1.57 = $408K

This is a 5-minute back-of-envelope, accurate to ±10% in most cases. Refine before closing with 3 actual comps + adjustments. Don't underwrite based on assessor alone.

**Why wholesalers love selling above assessor:** they know the gap. If the property assesses at $200K and they're listing at $280K, the spread looks shocking — but real ARV may be $360-380K, making the deal compelling.

Use this technique to catch deals where the headline price looks high but the math actually works.

## Day 14 — Florida probate court calendar + off-market deals

Probate is one of the most consistent off-market deal sources in Florida. Here's why:

- ~150,000 FL probate filings/year, statewide
- Of those, roughly 30-40% involve real property
- Heirs typically want the property sold within 6-12 months
- Many heirs live out-of-state and can't manage the property
- Result: motivated sellers willing to discount for cash + speed

**Where to find them:**

Each FL county clerk has a public probate calendar. Search by:
- "Letters of Administration" filings
- Cases with "Estate" in the case caption
- Recently filed (last 60-90 days) — heirs are still organizing

**The play:**

1. Pull weekly list of new probate filings (free; varies by county portal)
2. Cross-reference with property records to find ones with real estate
3. Send personal letter (NOT a corporate mailer) to the personal representative
4. Wait 3-6 weeks; ~3-7% will respond
5. Of responders, ~30-50% lead to a discounted off-market deal

**Common pitfalls:**

- Waiting too long: by month 6, the heirs have hired an agent
- Generic mailers: investors saturate this market; standout requires personalization
- Not understanding the process: probate sales take 60-90 days even when motivated; pricing should reflect that
- Multiple heirs disagreeing on price (very common — has killed many of mine)
