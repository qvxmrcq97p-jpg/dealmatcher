## Broward

**Did you know?** Fort Lauderdale earned the nickname "Venice of America" because of its 165 miles of navigable inland waterways — more canal frontage than any U.S. city except its big sister down the highway, Cape Coral. That's why SFR pricing in Broward bifurcates so sharply: a waterfront lot east of Federal Highway can clear 40-60% over a comparable inland comp three streets back.

The inland value-add band (zips 33305, 33311, 33319, 33069) is the working playbook today: 1950s-60s CBS construction, lots under 8,000 SF, most of the rehab risk priced into the entry. The classic Broward fix-and-flip math is a 988 SF fixer in 33311 with an ARV ~$365K — tight spread, fast turn, in and out in 90 days. Insurance is the deal-killer; a roof inside the carrier's window (under 12 years on standard, under 18 on Citizens) is what makes the math work.

## Miami-Dade

**Did you know?** The Miami-Dade NW corridor — zips 33125, 33127, 33135, 33142, 33147, 33150 — was developed almost entirely between 1925 and 1955. After Hurricane Andrew (1992) gutted southern parts of the county, the building code overhaul that followed (and the 2002 unified Florida Building Code) made teardown-and-replace economics painful in NW Miami: most properties there predate the modern code, so a substantial rehab triggers full code-compliance review. That's why so many deals in the corridor are priced as fixers rather than flips — buyers are pricing in the permit friction.

Allapattah (33142) is the corridor's gentrification edge. Wynwood-adjacent investor money has been flowing west of I-95 since 2018; today's 33142 fixers at sub-$400K Asking with ARVs in the $470K–$525K band reflect that thesis, not random wholesale margins. Liberty City (33147) is one cycle behind on appreciation but the same playbook is starting to print there. Coconut Grove (33133), Miami-Dade's oldest continuously inhabited neighborhood, has thinned to ~10 active SFR listings in the value-add 2-3 bed band — days-on-market in 33133 is averaging 38 vs. 67 for Miami-Dade overall. Anything below $500K with a real ARV spread is getting multiple offers within 72 hours.

**Insurance shift:** Citizens depopulation pulled roughly 31,400 Miami-Dade policies into private carriers in Q1. If you bought a SFR between 2020 and 2024, your renewal this spring may quietly drop 12-18% — the depopulation push is moving lower-risk policies off Citizens and back into competitive private carriers like Universal and Tower Hill. Flip economics that didn't pencil at the 2024 carrier rates may pencil now.

## Palm Beach

**Did you know?** Palm Beach County has the largest gap between its priciest and cheapest census tracts of any FL county — over $4M between median sale price in the town of Palm Beach (33480) and the western glades communities (33430). For investors, this means the 33401/33405/33409 belt sits in a productive middle: close enough to Worth Avenue money to support flip exits, far enough out for entries that pencil.

The Treasure Coast just north (St. Lucie + Martin + Indian River) has been the fastest-appreciating non-tri-county FL corridor since 2022. Port St. Lucie ranked #1 U.S. city for net per-capita in-migration through 2024 — the same wave hitting eastern Palm Beach.

## Hillsborough

**Did you know?** Tampa MSA was the #1 U.S. metro for net-migration in 2023 and held the #2 spot in 2024. But median sale price is actually flat year-over-year and days-on-market has more than doubled from the 2022 peak. Sellers who bought near the peak are first-time price-cutters — the $200K–$350K band is where motivated supply finally exceeds investor demand.

Forest Hills (33612) is the canonical value-add belt: 1960s CBS construction, comparable 4/2 SFH retail flips closing $480K–$520K. Median sale price across Hillsborough today: $385K, down 5% vs. the 2022 peak. Days on market: 51 days, up 27 days YoY. The deal flow is finally turning in the investor's favor.

## Pinellas

**Did you know?** Pinellas is Florida's densest county and 100% built-out — every transaction is a renovation play. Property tax burden per assessed dollar is the lowest in the state. The numbers: median home value ~$425K (flat YoY), $/sqft in 33713 up 31% vs. the 2019 baseline, population density 3,400/sq mi (highest in FL).

Post-Helene and post-Milton (Oct 2024), Flood Zone X properties — no flood insurance required — have transacted at roughly 12 points stronger pricing than otherwise comparable AE-zone properties. A 2025 roof on a block-built SFH eliminates the typical insurance-carrier objection at closing, which is the single biggest deal-killer in FL right now. The St. Petersburg I-75 corridor properties built before 1965 with block construction and a recent roof are the cleanest investor entries on the entire peninsula.

## Pasco

**Did you know?** Pasco County's population grew 18% in five years — third-fastest in Florida, outpacing every metro county except Orange. The I-75 corridor through Zephyrhills, Wesley Chapel, and Land O' Lakes holds one of the last sub-$200K single-family price bands on the FL peninsula.

The Pasco bottom quartile — older block construction, garage, 1/4-acre lot — is where most of the value-add cash flow lives. A 1,200 SF flip-or-hold like Zephyrhills 33541 at ~$172K is the canonical Pasco entry. Rent comps run $1,500–$1,900 for 2/2s and 3/2s. The 1-percent rule still works in Pasco, where it gave up the ghost in Hillsborough five years ago.

## Orange

**Did you know?** Orange County added an Orlando metro that's now the third-largest tourism economy in the world — but the rental thesis for investors isn't Disney. The corridor between Pine Hills (32808), Holden Heights (32805), and Hiawassee (32811) is where Section 8 rents on 3/2 SFR cleared $2,200/month in 2024, with entry prices still in the $180K–$240K band. Median ask across Orange today is $209K — investor-friendly.

## Lee

**Did you know?** Cape Coral has more canals than any other U.S. city — about 400 miles of them — laid out by the Rosen brothers' Gulf American Corporation in 1957. The original GAC plan subdivided 120 square miles into roughly 1/4-acre lots, sold mail-order to retirees who often never visited. By the late 1960s the Rosens went bust under FTC fraud charges, but the grid stayed. That's why, six decades later, ~70% of Cape Coral's land area is still buildable: vacant inventory from the original GAC subdivision.

Hurricane Ian (September 2022) reset the SW Florida market — and Lee County in particular. Coastal-zone values cratered, then partially rebuilt as insurance-reform legislation cleared in 2023 and the build-permit pipeline finally unclogged. Today's Cape Coral lots in 33993/33991/33909 trade $35K–$45K dirt, and build-to-rent operators (Lennar, Invitation Homes, regional BTR REITs) are quietly stockpiling ahead of the next post-storm migration wave. The honest math on a Cape Coral lot: $43K dirt + $200K–$300K turnkey build = $243K–$343K all-in vs. comparable built 3/2 SFH closing ~$300K. Margin depends entirely on builder spread, timeline, and retail-vs-rent exit. This is a land-basis play more than a flip play — different math, different timeline.

## St. Lucie

**Did you know?** Fort Pierce (34950) is the cheapest entry on the entire Florida Atlantic seaboard — median ask $250K. The 2-bed cottage band trades $150K–$220K, rent comps $1,650–$1,900, and the 1-percent rule still works there — almost nowhere else in coastal FL can claim that. Port St. Lucie ranked #1 U.S. city for net per-capita in-migration through 2024 per Census data.

Textbook BRRRR exits work here at entry points that are roughly 40% cheaper than Jacksonville. A 720 SF 2/1 cottage in 34950 with structurally sound bones (roof under 10 years, city sewer) is the canonical St. Lucie entry — sub-$200K asking, rent ~$1,750, refi at 70% LTV pulls most of the buy-and-rehab capital back out.

## Polk

**Did you know?** Polk County sits at the geographic center of the I-4 corridor — Tampa to the west, Orlando to the east — and median ask sits at $158K, among the lowest in any metro-adjacent FL county. Lakeland's 1950s-60s housing stock is concentrated in 33801, 33803, and 33805. Days-on-market doubled in 12 months. Polk is where the 2026 cash-flow thesis lives if you can wait three years for appreciation to catch up.

## Sarasota

**Did you know?** Sarasota County has the second-highest non-Miami median sale price in the state at $525K — but the variance under the median is huge. East Sarasota (34235) trades at less than half the coastal median. Mid-century FL block 1950s/1960s SFH inventory there comps retail-flip at $370K–$400K, with entries in the $200K–$280K band. That East Sarasota gap is where the math works.

## Manatee

**Did you know?** Manatee (Bradenton) is the lowest-priced of the four Tampa Bay counties on a per-square-foot basis — median ~$258K. Sub-tri-county pricing, with Tampa-area economic gravity. The 34203/34207 belt is the active investor band.

## Collier

**Did you know?** Collier County (Naples/Marco Island) has the highest median home price in the state outside Palm Beach proper, but the eastern interior of the county — Golden Gate Estates and Immokalee — is a different market entirely. Lot sizes are huge (1.25–5 acres common), zoning is permissive, and entries on raw land sit $40K–$90K. Long horizon plays only; not flip territory.

## Charlotte

**Did you know?** Port Charlotte sits between Cape Coral and Sarasota geographically but operates economically like Lee County — Hurricane Ian reset values, insurance reform unclogged the pipeline. Median ask under $250K. The 33980/33952/33954 belt is the working investor zone, with mid-century block SFH entries in the $180K–$260K band.

## Duval

**Did you know?** Duval County (Jacksonville) added 22,000 net residents in 2024, the third-largest population gain of any FL county. Median ask $139K — Florida's cheapest big-county entry by a wide margin. The catch: Jacksonville's rent-to-price ratio isn't quite as friendly as St. Lucie or Pasco, so the BRRRR math is closer to the 0.8% rule than the 1% rule. But entry capital requirement is the lowest in the state.
